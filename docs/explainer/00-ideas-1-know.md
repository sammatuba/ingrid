# What we know

The explainer opens on four questions.

What do we know, why do we believe it, and when should it change? Given uncertainty, what should
we do, and what is timely information worth? Who may propose, accept, decide and act? How does an
outcome change beliefs, methods and goals?

This chapter takes the first question. The next three take the others. The system that carries all
four comes at the end of this part of the walk.

## The question

What do we know, why do we believe it, and when should it change?

## The idea, in everyday words

A belief is a fact that a person or a company treats as true. All work rests on beliefs. A claim
is what a source says, before anyone has checked it. A belief is a claim the company has accepted.

An example. A courier company plans a route over a bridge. The plan rests on one belief: the
bridge is open today. A driver's morning report is the source of that belief. The belief holds
for today, and no longer. When the company hears that the bridge has closed, the belief changes,
and the plan changes with it.

## The desk's word

The desk calls a belief an assumption, and it keeps the source and the time span with the
assumption. What a notice says, before the check, is the claim.
