# How we learn

This chapter takes the fourth question, and then hands the walk to the desk.

## The question

How does an outcome change beliefs, methods and goals?

## The idea, in everyday words

A forecast meets the world, and the world answers. The gap between the two is the score. A score
on its own changes nothing, so the desk asks what caused it.

An example. A delivery company expects a quiet Monday, so it plans two vans. Monday is heavy,
and parcels wait a day. The company measures the gap. Then it asks why: was the belief about
Mondays wrong, did a signal arrive late, or was the plan itself wrong?

A miss can correct the plan or the belief about Mondays: that is the first loop. A miss that
repeats can change the method, the way the company plans, or the goal behind it: that is the
second loop.

## The bridge into the desk

One outage, named by layer. This is a proposed walk: no case in the repository has run it whole.
The status of each step follows it.

- **Knowledge.** An operator notice arrives, and the desk records what it says. Done once, on the
  real case.
- **Judgment.** The model answers the fixed questions, and the analyst weighs the cost and decides.
  The model ran once on the real case. The price effect of this outage is not computed, and the
  real case holds no analyst decision.
- **Work.** Any action that follows the decision needs its own approval and leaves a receipt. Out
  of the desk's scope.
- **Learning.** When the month settles, the outcome scores the forecast. The desk has scored no
  real month yet.

The desk chapters that follow take this walk one step at a time.
