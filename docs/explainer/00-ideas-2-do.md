# What we do

This chapter takes the second question.

## The question

Given uncertainty, what should we do, and what is timely information worth?

## The idea, in everyday words

A forecast says what we expect to happen. A decision says what we will do about it. The desk
keeps the two apart, because one is a claim about the world and the other is an act.

An example. A shopkeeper expects a hot weekend, so she orders ice. The forecast can be wrong, and
the order still has to go in. Her cost comes in her own units: kilos of ice bought and left
unsold. She sets a line in advance, and the order changes only when the forecast crosses it.
After the weekend she compares what sold with what she expected, and keeps the score.

Timing matters too. A warning on Friday is worth more to her than the same warning on Saturday
night.

## The desk's word

The desk calls a forecast an outlook, and it calls the cost of a change the impact, counted in the
company's own units. The table of scores is the scorecard.

## Where it is thin

The desk gives a change a peso figure and a line, and it computes no expected value over the
choices. It measures how late a signal arrives, not what an earlier signal is worth.
