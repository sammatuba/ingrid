## The meta problem above the whiteboard

Added 2026-10-17 by the [meta framing record](../work/meta-framing-2026-10-17/strategy.md). The
whiteboard below is the domain application. The problem above it is an AI problem: **who answers
for a decision made with AI?** AI can read every signal and draft every change; people still decide
and still answer for the result. An organization that cannot show which AI judgment it trusted, and
why, holds AI back or uses it blind. The whiteboard's headline is that problem in the market's words.

## Headline

> **From Market Signals to Trustworthy, Traceable Decisions.**

## Problem statement

1. **Evidence-backed, quick decisions.**
2. **Organize cluttered information.**
3. These two lead to: **connecting market information to portfolio impact, leading to a board
   decision.**
4. **Material market changes affecting the portfolio and the outlook:**
   `D - S - P - O - F(Bid), Rules`: demand, supply, price, outage and fuel (bid), plus the rules
   (policies and market rules). Confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-17; the letter read earlier as
   `IPs[?]` is `O` for outage. The same board was transcribed on 2026-10-16 in
   [problem and thesis](../work/thesis-challenge-2026-10-16/problem-and-thesis.md), which is the
   authority for how the board becomes the price chain; there, read P as price, not portfolio.
5. **Establishing causality of WESM factors to the result.**
6. **Regulatory rules, policy-driven incentives and goals influencing price results.**
7. **Review of past decisions for future action recommendations.**

## Solutions offered

1. **AI trustability.**
   - Counters human error.
   - Offers an ontological framework.
   - **Challenge:** How can we show the user that they can trust the AI?
2. **Traceability.**
   - Avoid the blame game.
   - Decision review.
3. **Transform decisions** from reactive, intuition-driven judgement into objective,
   portfolio-aware actions, grounded in complex market intelligence and fully traceable and
   trustworthy evidence.
